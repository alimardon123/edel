# shell-ui

`edel-shell-ui`: the Edel shell's panels and menus (ADR-002), the second of its two long-running processes. Edition 2024, `rust-version = "1.85"`, GPL-3.0-or-later, `publish = false`.

## What belongs here

- What people use to find and switch things: the panel (M5.1b), its workspace switcher (M5.2c), window list (M5.2h) and layout toggle (M5.3a), the launcher (M5.3b) and the window switcher's list (M5.3c), and later the tray (M5.2e), notifications and quick settings (M5.9), the lock screen and greeter (M5.10). Windows, title bars and effects belong to the compositor; settings pages to Settings.
- It draws its own surfaces (ADR-002's shell-ui toolkit decision of 2026-10-03): layer-shell surfaces through smithay-client-toolkit, drawing with tiny-skia into shared memory, text shaped by cosmic-text. No GTK, no GLib, no scripting, no theme engine. [docs/REVIEW-shells.md](../../docs/REVIEW-shells.md) is what its look and motion learn from.
- Colours and sizes come only from the design tokens (`edel::tokens`); never hard-code one in drawing code.
- Redraw only what changed, and only when it changed: an idle panel draws nothing between minutes.

## Layout

- `main.rs`: the Wayland connection; the preset (`shell.preset` from the machine's and the person's system file through `edel::system`, else Classic, read once at start through `edel::presets`) and one layer surface per panel in it (namespace `edel-panel`, along its edge, exclusive zone `size.panel`, a `size.radius` strip on its inner side for the fillets that is neither opaque nor clickable); the calloop loop with the minute timer that redraws the panels; buffers from a `SlotPool`; each panel's scale from `preferred_buffer_scale`; the tier from the compositor's state file (fillets unless Lite); a pointer from the seat, whose left clicks and scrolls go to the widget under them (`Shell::input`, by the places `paint` returned), and the `Live` state widgets show, kept by `workspaces_changed` and `windows_changed`; scrolls add up in `widgets::Scrolled` (a high-resolution wheel's 120ths, a touchpad's pixels) until they make whole steps; the launcher's surface (`Menu`: a `Popup` named `edel-launcher`, anchored beside the first panel's start, 8 px from the panel and the side, with the keyboard alone) and the seat's keyboard (sctk's xkbcommon, with repeat), all made in `open_launcher` and let go in `close_launcher`, which the keyboard leaving also calls; keys go to `launcher_key`, the pointer's motion and clicks on it to its rows; it logs `launcher shown, N apps`, `launched NAME (COMMAND)` and `launcher hidden`; each change of where widgets lie is logged as `panel places NAME X+W, ...` in logical pixels, which CI reads to click them. It exits when the compositor goes away or takes a panel away; the compositor starts it again (`shellui.rs` there).
- `paint.rs`: `Look` (everything a panel shows at one scale, what each widget shows included), `Row` (the panel's widgets, from its start, in its centre and towards its end) and `paint`, which draws a panel into a tiny-skia pixmap (its colour, the fillets on either edge and the widgets laid out in their groups) and returns where each widget lies; `Text` (cosmic-text's fonts and glyph cache, loaded once, in the tokens' `font.interface` family) shapes and draws a `Line`, and `fit` cuts one short with an ellipsis; `mix` dims a colour towards another; `lit` is the accent at 18%, under a chosen row or a switched-on button; `rounded` is a rounded rectangle path and `fill` fills one; `to_argb` gives `wl_shm`'s byte order. Tested without a display.
- `widgets/mod.rs` (M5.1c): the widget table. A widget is one module here and one line in `TABLE`: its name (what presets write), the feature it needs if any, and four functions: what it shows now, from `Live` (the panel redraws only when that changes), its width and how it draws, on a `Canvas`, both from what it shows, and what an `Input` (a click with the widget's width, a scroll) on it does, an `Action` (show a workspace, move the switcher's view, activate or minimize a window) or nothing (`no_input`); and its `role` and `label` for screen readers (M5.1d). `usable` keeps the widgets a preset names that this machine can show and notes the rest: a name the table lacks, or a widget whose feature has no `/usr/share/edel/features/NAME.toml`. The tests fail when a line needs a feature with no file under `features/` or a built-in preset names a widget the table lacks.
- `a11y.rs` (M5.1d): what screen readers read. `tree` makes each panel a window in an AccessKit tree whose children are its widgets of some width, with the role and label from each widget's line (a label's text is its value) and their places from `paint`; a `Reader` per panel holds an `accesskit_unix::Adapter`, which serves the tree over AT-SPI on the session's D-Bus only once a reader turns accessibility on (`org.a11y.Status`). Until then `update` keeps the panel's widgets and builds no tree. Widgets take no actions from a reader yet. `ci/desktop/features/desktop-test/usr/libexec/edel-a11y-tree` is the test's screen reader. Tested.
- `widgets/menu.rs`: the menu button's icon, four rounded squares in a square as tall as the panel; a click opens or closes the launcher (M5.3b).
- `popup.rs` (M5.3): what the launcher and the switcher share. `Popup` is a surface of its own on the overlay layer with its own `SlotPool`, made when shown and dropped when hidden: `canvas` gives a pixmap only when configured and what it shows changed, `show` attaches it, `resize` asks for a new size. `card` draws the menus' card (the panel's colour, `size.radius` corners) and `rows` a list of labels `size.row` high, the chosen one `lit` with `size.radius_control` corners. A change to those tokens reaches both. Tested.
- `launcher.rs` (M5.3b): the launcher's look and state. `paint` draws a `View` (what was typed, the matching names, the chosen row) at `size`: a `popup::card`, a search line with the caret in the accent, eight `popup::rows`; `Launcher` reads the apps on `open`, forgets them on `close`, searches as people type, steps the choice and `start`s an app with its stdio closed in the home directory, keeping the child until `reap` (on each opening, closing and minute) collects it. Tested.
- `switcher.rs` (M5.3c): the window switcher's picture. `View` is the compositor's titles and chosen place; `paint` draws a `WIDTH` (420 px) `popup::card` whose `size` follows the titles, at most `MOST` (10), as `popup::rows`. `main.rs` makes its surface (a `Popup` named `edel-switcher` with no anchor, so in the middle, and no keyboard) on the first `switcher` event, sizes it again when the number of titles changes and lets it all go on `switcher_hide`, logging `switcher shown, N windows` and `switcher hidden`. Tested.
- `apps.rs` (M5.3b): the `.desktop` files, read without GLib: `dirs` (`XDG_DATA_HOME` or `~/.local/share`, `XDG_DATA_DIRS` or `/usr/local/share:/usr/share`, then Flatpak's exports), `read_all` (the first file with an id wins, sorted by name), `parse` (`[Desktop Entry]` applications that are shown, `OnlyShowIn` and `NotShowIn` against `Edel`, string escapes), `split_exec` (quotes and field codes), `search` (name itself, name start, word start, then name, keyword or command) and `command` (terminal apps in foot). Tested.
- `widgets/workspaces.rs` (M5.2c): the workspace switcher. It shows `FIRST;NAME,NAME*,...`: at most three round buttons (`SHOWN`), the shown workspace a wider pill in `colour.accent`, the others filled with the panel text's colour at 14 percent, and a dot at a side where more lie; its width stays the same as the shown workspace changes. A click shows the workspace under it, a scroll moves the view, kept in sight. A screen reader hears "Workspaces: 2 shown, 2 of 4". Tested.
- `widgets/layout.rs` (M5.3a): the layout toggle. It shows the shown workspace's policy; one 30 by 26 px button, two overlapping windows while floating, a screen split in three on an accent fill while tiling, its icon drawn from the mockups' 24-unit square; a click asks `link` to switch. Tested.
- `link.rs` (M5.3a): the client of `edel-shell-v1`, the compositor's private protocol, generated by `wayland-scanner` from `../compositor/protocols/edel-shell-v1.xml`: the policy event into `Live::policy` and `toggle_policy`.
- `widgets/windows.rs` (M5.2h): the window list. It shows a line per window on a screen, `*TITLE` for the focused one, `-TITLE` for a minimized one, ` TITLE` else; every button is one width (`button_width`: at most 180 px, together at most 45% of the panel), its title cut with an ellipsis; when even the narrowest, 32 px, do not fit, `buttons` gives as many as do, the last counting the windows left over (`+N`) and bringing the first of them forward; the focused one is lit with a 14 px accent line along its foot, others have a dot, a minimized one dimmer text and no mark. A click on the focused window minimizes it, on another activates it. A screen reader hears "Windows: Files (focused), Mail (minimized)". Tested.
- `toplevels.rs` (M5.2h): the wlr-foreign-toplevel-management client, through `Dispatch2` user data (`Manager`, `Handle`): each window's title, screens, focus and minimized state; `tasks` gives the windows on a screen, in order, which `activate` (with the seat) and `minimize` address by place; `windows_changed` at each `done` and `closed`; without the protocol the list shows nothing.
- `workspaces.rs` (M5.2c): the ext-workspace-v1 client, through sctk's `Dispatch2` user data (`Manager`, `Group`, `Handle`): the workspaces by name with the shown one marked, `show` (activate, then commit), and `workspaces_changed` at each `done`; a workspace or group removed is destroyed; without the protocol the switcher shows nothing.
- `widgets/clock.rs`: the clock, `HH:MM` with room on both sides, and the time until the next minute. Tested.
- `features/NAME.rs` (none yet): code that talks to an OS feature, such as NetworkManager for `network` (M5.9); its widget's line says `needs: Some("NAME")`.

## Running it

Inside a running compositor: `WAYLAND_DISPLAY=wayland-N cargo run -p edel-shell-ui`. The compositor starts it by itself when `edel-shell-ui` is on the `PATH`, as in the desktop image (the `shell` feature).

# The modules of the compositor

What each module holds, one bullet per module (moved here from [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) by M6.17, line 11, so the rules a session reads stay short). Read the bullets of the modules you change before editing them, and keep them true in the same PR: a new module adds its bullet, a removed one takes its bullet out, a changed one edits only its own bullet.

- `lib.rs`: the parts that need no display, tested with `cargo test`:
  - `tokens`: re-exports `edel::tokens` (the design tokens moved to the `edel` library in M5.1b, so shell-ui reads them with the same code).
  - `messages.rs` (M5.28b): what the compositor says when something fails, one function or constant per message, in the words of `docs/MESSAGES.md` (what failed, why, what to do or what still works), with the tests that hold their text; a new error message is written here, routine status lines that CI greps are not.
  - `overview.rs` (M5.2j, M5.2j-b): the overview's geometry, pure and tested: `Side` (`workspaces.overview_strip`, `named`, the left by default) and `plan`, which stands every workspace's small frame (11 percent of the screen's width down a side and 13.5 across, as the mockup draws them, never under `FRAME_DOWN` 72 px or `FRAME_ACROSS` 96 nor over twice them, the screen's shape, a `LABEL` row under each) and the frame that adds one in a strip on a tray (`TRAY_PAD`, `TRAY_EDGE` from the free area's edge, `TRAY_RADIUS`), centred along its side, and gives the stage, the rest less `ROOM`; when the items do not fit along the side (M5.2j-b2), the frames keep their size and the strip shows as many as fit from `first` on, between two `ARROW` rooms (`before`, `after` where more lie, `scrolls`), a frame out of view an empty rectangle and the add frame none, and `in_view` keeps one item in view moving as little as it can; the search field (M5.2j-b3, `search`, `SEARCH_WIDE` by `SEARCH_HIGH`) takes the stage's top, centred, `ROOM` from the tray and from the windows under it; the tray's first row is its `caption`, `CAPTION` high, which reads "Workspaces" in small, quiet letters, so the strip names itself; `spread` lays the shown workspace's windows out on the stage in the grid of columns that shows them largest, at one scale never above their own size, `NAME_ROOM` under each; `scale`, `into` and `shrink` map a point or a window's place from the screen into a frame, `frame_at` finds the frame under the pointer, and `frames` is M5.2j-a's row of frames; `tip` stands a frame's tooltip beside the tray, level with the frame (M5.2j-b4), and `swiped` says whether a touchpad swipe opens or leaves the overview (four fingers, `SWIPE` px up or down). `glance.rs` draws it.
  - `telemetry.rs`: render times and idle frames; `percentile` is nearest rank, the same rule as CI's desktop test (M4.1); `Summary` prints `frames N, render p50 X ms, p99 Y ms, idle frames Z`.
  - `layout.rs`: the `WindowPolicy` trait (`open`, `close`, `moved`, `arrange`, `rearranges`), `policies`, the table of every policy (one line each), `Workspace` (M5.2g: on each screen, by its name, every policy follows that screen's windows and the active one places them in that screen's area, so windows tile and maximize on their own screen; `switch` and `next` change the policy for every screen; `screen_of` says where a window lies; a floating window `moved` onto another screen moves to it; `arrange(areas)` moves the windows of a screen no longer there to the first), `Floating` (M4.3: centred, cascading `CASCADE` px off a window with the same centre, kept inside the area; the CI pixel checks depend on this rule) and `OutputLayout` (mode, scale snapped to 1/120, logical size). Policies place frames, not windows.
  - `layout.rs` also has `auto_scale` (M4.6a: the EDID rule) and `scale_120` and `snap_scale` (every scale in 1/120 steps from 1 to 4).
  - `cursor.rs` (M4.6b): `arrow`, the pointer's arrow drawn at any size, for when no xcursor theme is installed.
  - `game.rs` (M7.7): game mode's rule, pure and tested: `When` (`power.game_mode`: `plugged-in`, the default, `always`, `off`) and `GameMode`, whose `on` asks for the battery only when the rule needs it and whose `toggle` is Super+G's choice over the key until the next login or the key's change. `whole_upscale` (M8.28) says whether a game's surface is shown at a whole number of times the size it drew, the same both ways, which `sharp.rs` reads.
  - `frame_times.rs` (M8.27): `Recent`, a game's last frames for the frame rate overlay, pure and tested: `shown` takes each frame's page flip time, keeping the last `KEPT` (5 s, at most 2048 frames); `shown_now` gives `Shown`, the frames of the last second (`fps`), their average time (`frame_ms`), the slowest 1 percent of the kept frames by nearest rank, and the last `GRAPHED` (120) frame times; `Shown::lines` is the overlay's two lines, numbers and units only.
  - `effects.rs` (M5.11a): `Tier` (Lite, Balanced, Full), `starting_tier` (Lite on a software renderer such as llvmpipe, at most Balanced on battery, else Full) and `Deadline`, which drops one tier when 5 of the last 60 frames took over three quarters of the screen's refresh interval, then counts afresh and never raises it within a session; tested.
  - `animation.rs` (M5.11b): `Motion` (`appearance.animations`: full, reduced, off), `style` (how long windows open, close and slide, and the size they grow from, by tier and motion: the "Window animations" default row), `Anim` (eased out progress on the frame clock, never running long when a frame is late), `opening` and `closing` (alpha and size) and `Slide` and `Spring` (M5.14: an offset from the window's place that a damped spring pulls to nothing; a spring keeps its position and speed when retargeted, so an interrupted slide carries on from where it is drawn; smooth and snappy are critically damped, snappy 1.5 times stiffer, springy damped at 0.7); `Switch` (M5.14) is one spring per screen that a workspace switch's incoming windows and leaving pictures share, which three fingers hold and move one to one (`swipe_offset`, `swipe_goes`, `SWIPE_SPAN`, `SWIPE_START`). Opening and closing are eased by their curve (`Curve`, `curve`: smooth, snappy or springy, M5.12b4; `Feel` gives each kind, windows, workspaces and popups, its curve from the one named, M8.21 part 21e; `style` takes the speed, `appearance.animation_speed` as a percent of the release's, and the curve); tested.
  - `corners.rs` (M5.14, built in M8.21 part 21c): `window_radius` (the `radius_window` token on Full and Balanced, square on Lite, maximized, fullscreen and tiled flush), `surface_radii` (a window's clipped corners, square under a bar), `corner_squares` (the corners opaque regions leave out), `uv_to_px`, and the text of `CLIP_SHADER` and `RING_SHADER` with their uniform names; tested.
  - `blur.rs` (M5.14, built in M8.21 part 21d): the live blur's rules, `passes` (halvings by `appearance.blur`), `offset`, `reach` (how far a change still changes the blur), `halved` and `levels` (the sizes of its pictures), and the text of the dual Kawase `DOWN_SHADER` and `UP_SHADER` with `BLUR_UNIFORMS`; tested.
  - `everyday.rs` (M5.13b, with M5.23): keyboard shortcuts inhibit, `KeyboardShortcutsInhibitHandler` on smithay's `KeyboardShortcutsInhibitState` (the `keyboard_shortcuts_inhibit` field of `state.rs`): a new inhibitor is activated at once and logged `shortcuts inhibited for TITLE`, its end logged `shortcuts back`; `input.rs` reads the focused surface's active inhibitor. The rest is M5.23's: pointer constraints (`hold_on`, `constrained`: a locked pointer stays put, a confined one stays over its window, while `input.rs` still sends the relative motion), relative pointer, primary selection, idle inhibit (`idle_inhibitors` in the state file), ext-idle-notify (M7.8: `idle_notifier`, which `sync_idle_inhibit` holds off after every dispatch while a surface inhibits, so a surface that died counts too) and xdg-activation (`bring_forward`, shared with the window list's activate). `screens_off_asked` and `screens_on` (M7.16b): shell-ui asks for the screens to go off after the idle time, a game playing on a screen refuses (a guard only: shell-ui asks for nothing then), and the next input turns them on again.
  - `sandbox.rs` (M5.22): `wp_security_context_v1`; `shell_may` hides the layer shell, the window list, the workspaces, `edel-shell-v1`, the clipboard's data control (M7.13) and the security context manager from a client a sandbox marked, through smithay's layer shell filter and our globals' `can_view`. The screen copy (`screencopy.rs`, M6.3) is hidden the same way, so a sandboxed app takes a picture only through the portal. Sockets a sandbox asks for are served from the event loop's handle, which `state::listen` keeps.
  - `tiling.rs` (M4.5, M5.16): `Tiling`, the one tiling policy, laying windows out in the `Style` that `layout.tiling_style` names: `stack` (master and stack with the `gap` and `gap_outer` tokens, as `layout::Gaps` (M5.12b2b: `inner` between windows, `outer` at the area's edge; `Gaps::within` drops each where it leaves no room); `tiles` is the geometry, tested for 1 to 6 windows), `split` (`split.rs`, a tree of halves, as Hyprland tiles) `scroll` (`scroll.rs`, a `Strip` of columns scrolled to the focused one, as niri tiles) or `full` (M9.4b, every window the whole area, the one in front all that shows, as on a phone). The policy interface's `focused`, `swap`, `widen` and `set_style` reach them; `layout::toward` finds the window Super with an arrow goes to.
  - `desks.rs` (M5.2a, M5.2i, M5.2k): `Desks`, the workspaces: each a `Workspace` of policies, its name (M5.2i: `set_names`, `labels` (the name, else the number), `names`; a name stays with its workspace when others close) and, while hidden, its windows bottom first with their frames. `add`, `remove` (an empty, unshown workspace; the shown one stays shown), `window_counts`, `is_empty` and `neighbour` (`step`, no wrap) serve dynamic workspaces, whose `dynamic_plan` (pure, tested) says which empty, unshown, unnamed workspaces close and whether one empty workspace waits at the end, at most `MOST`; `switch` hides the shown windows and hands back the others, `send` moves a shown window to a hidden workspace's top, placed by its policy there, `set_count` follows the preset (the windows of workspaces that go join the last that stays; never the shown one, which the compositor leaves first); every window keeps its screen (`screen_in` finds it in the areas, else the first; with no screen at all, its frame on its own screen until one comes); `minimize` takes a shown window out of the policies and keeps it on its workspace with its frame, `restore` gives it back to them where it was, `minimized` says where one waits, and `hidden` and `hidden_on` count minimized windows as off screen (M5.2h); tested. With `workspaces.per_screen` (M5.2k: `set_per_screen`), each screen shows its own workspace (`shown_on`, `shown`), the pointer's screen's in use (`use_screen`, `active`); `switch_on` switches one screen, `arrange` lays out each screen's own workspace there, `desk_of`, `layout_of` and `layout_on_mut` find a window's or a screen's workspace, and `settle_screens` moves a window put on another screen to the workspace shown there and brings back hidden windows whose screen shows their workspace (after a screen came or went). `open_on` (M5.2l) opens a new window on a named workspace through that workspace's policies, and says whether it shows now or waits hidden on its workspace. `carry` moves a window to a workspace on its own screen or, dropped on another screen's frame in the overview, on that one (M5.2j-b4). `reorder` (M5.2p) moves a workspace to another place with its windows, name and policies, every screen keeping the workspace it showed; `reordered_names` and `names_to_write` give the person's `workspaces.names` after such a move (nothing when there are no names, the person's line reset when it equals the machine's).
  - `settings.rs` (M4.5): `Settings` (`tiling`, `preset`, `title_bars` and `tiling_style` as the files and the preset give them, resolved once in `from_files` (M5.18b), `window_buttons` and `buttons` (M6.16: both from `edel::presets::buttons_applying`, the one rule shell-ui's apps share), `panels`, `outputs`, `motion`, `color_scheme`, `accent` and `colours` (M5.12b1), `shape` (M5.12b2b, the gaps, border and title bar, which `state.rs` follows live), `shortcuts`) from the machine's and the person's settings file through `edel::settings`, the person's keys winning; `policy` is `layout.tiling` if set, else the preset's (`edel::presets`, M5.1c), and `workspaces` `workspaces.count` if set, else the preset's count (M5.2a, M5.2i); `dynamic()` (`workspaces.dynamic`) and `names()` (`workspaces.names`, M5.2i), `workspace_wallpapers` (`workspaces.wallpapers`, M5.12b5, the whole list, by place); `load` reads both leniently and notes a preset this release lacks. `app_workspaces` and `app_workspace` (M5.2l) give each app id's workspace from `workspaces.apps`, the person's table replacing the machine's whole.
  - `frame.rs` (M4.4): server-side frames, drawn in screen pixels at the output's scale (M4.6a). `Insets` (the bar above, the border round the rest) turns a window's place into its frame's and back; the bar draws a hairline (`line`) under itself and the window's border along its top and sides, `appearance.border_width` (`tokens.border`) px wide and in `window_border` or, focused, `window_border_focused` (a gradient to `window_border_focused_end` across the frame, `frame::Border`), none when maximized; the side and bottom borders take the same colours, the bottom as a texture when it is a gradient (M5.12b2b). `Insets` keeps the bar's own height in `bar` (the buttons' and title's size) and adds `B - 1` above it, so a border of 1 is the old frame; `Look::icon` and `paint`'s picture (M5.6a) put the app's icon left of the title, the two centred together; `Shown` (M5.18a) says which buttons the bars show, from `layout.close_button`, `minimize_button` and `maximize_button`, and `buttons(side, shown)` lists them from the bar's end, a hidden one leaving no gap and its room to the title; `Edel::shown` (M6.18) hands a window with a parent, a dialog, only close; `hit` says which part of a frame a point is on (title, minimize, maximize, close, an edge or corner within `GRIP` px); `paint` draws a bar described by a `Look` into ARGB pixels, its buttons' icons from `design/icons/` (`close`, `maximize`, `restore`, `minimize`) through `edel::icons::mask`, half the button wide (M5.5d); `Text` is the title font (`fontdue`) with its letters cached: `load` takes the tokens' `font.interface` family, finds its file among the system's fonts (`font_file`: `XDG_DATA_DIRS`' `fonts` folders, its variable file, then its regular one) and falls back to the first of `FONTS` that loads (`edel::places::FALLBACK_FONTS`, which shell-ui reads too, M6.17).
- `main.rs`: arguments (`--bench`, or `-- PROGRAM ARG...`), the tokens from `/usr/share/edel/design/tokens.toml`, else the built-in ones, and the backend: winit when built with the `winit` feature and `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` or `DISPLAY` is set, else DRM (a build without it says it has no development window and draws on the graphics card). A person's session (DRM, no `--bench`, no program, so not the greeter) first sends standard output and error, its own and everything it starts, to the person's session log (`edel::session_log::start`, M5.28a), and ends it with `edel::session_log::ENDED` when it closes as it should.
- `state.rs`: `Edel`, the Wayland state: compositor, shm, xdg-shell (offering maximize only), xdg-decoration and KDE's server decoration, seat, data device, outputs, presentation time, one `Space` with the shown workspace's windows and `desks`, the workspaces (M5.2a); `dirty` says something changed. `keyboard_shortcuts_inhibit` is the smithay global for a client's request to have its keys without ours (M5.13b, `everyday.rs`). `pending_copies` holds the screen copies clients asked for until `screencopy.rs` fills them, and `copy_clients` the clients whose first copy was logged (M6.3). A new toplevel waits in `unplaced` until its first buffer, then the policy places its frame and it takes the keyboard; `placed`, `outputs_changed` and `state_changed` keep the policy, the space and the state file in step, and a window that resizes itself or changes its title writes the state again; a window that drops its buffer leaves the screen and goes back to `unplaced`, to be placed anew when it draws again. `put` places a frame and tells the window its size and whether it is tiled, unless the window is on a hidden workspace; `relayout` puts every shown window where the active policy says and keeps their stacking order, after a switch (`switch_policy`, for every workspace, and Super+Y's `toggle_tiling`, for the shown one), an output change or, in tiling, an open or close. `window_of` finds hidden windows too; `state_toml` writes `playing` and the process's `pid` (M7.7c), and `state_changed` logs a game's start and end; `focus` calls `state_changed`, as a raise may put a window on top of a game (M7.7c). one that closes or drops its buffer while hidden leaves its workspace and the state file, and the state file lists the shown workspace's windows, then the hidden ones, each with its `workspace`. `dragging` is set while a `WindowGrab` runs: never ask the pointer `is_grabbed` from code a grab calls, as it waits on its own lock. `listen` opens the socket. A mapped window with an app id logs `window TITLE belongs to app ID` (M6.4), which desktop-test's `live` case reads for a web app. A new window whose app has a rule (`workspaces.apps`, M5.2l) is placed by `commit` on that workspace through `Desks::open_on`: shown, it is mapped and focused as any new window; when the workspace is not shown it stays unmapped on that workspace, and the person stays where they are. `screens_off` (M7.16b) is the flag `everyday.rs` sets, which the state file writes as `screens_off`; `saver` (M8.31) is the battery saver's flag, which the state file writes as `saver` beside `tier`.
- `extworkspace.rs` (M5.2b, M5.2i): ext-workspace-v1, for the panel's switcher and docks; a workspace is called by its name when it has one, else its number, and one closed in the middle renames the ones after it. `Managers` keeps each bound manager with its one group (no capabilities: the preset makes the workspaces), the workspace handles it was sent (user data: the number from 0) and the `wl_output`s the group entered; `announce_workspaces` sends what changed and `done` after a switch, a count change, an output change and each new `wl_output` (`OutputHandler::output_bound`). `activate` is remembered and applied by `switch_workspace` at the manager's `commit`; `stop` gets `finished`. With workspaces on each screen (M5.2o) each screen has its own group, entering that screen alone, its active workspace the one that screen shows, and an activate there switches that screen (`switch_screen`); when the key or the screens change, a manager's groups go and new ones come.
- `lock.rs` (M5.10a): ext-session-lock-v1, for shell-ui's lock screen alone (`sandbox::shell_may`). While `Edel::locked` holds (`Locked`: the lock object, each screen's `LockSurface` by name, a `SolidColorBuffer` cover per screen, the screens that drew locked), `render.rs` draws each screen's lock surface over the cover in the tokens' `lock_backdrop_deep` and nothing else (`lock_elements`), `input.rs` gives keys to the lock surface with no shortcut but the volume and brightness keys and the terminals, the pointer finds only the lock surface (`lock_under`) and a click reaches nothing else; `focus`, `focus_top`, `give_keyboard` and `focus_layer` leave the keyboard with the lock, the overview does not open and the switcher closes. The lock is confirmed to its client (`lock_drawn`) once every screen has drawn a locked frame, on the DRM backend once that frame is on the screen (its vblank, M7.8), so a suspend asked after the lock never sleeps on an unlocked picture; a second lock while a living one holds is refused, and one whose client died is replaced, as a restarted shell-ui is asked to lock again when it binds `edel-shell-v1` (`lock_orphaned`). `ask_lock` is Super+L (`Act::Lock`), which sends `lock` over `edel-shell-v1` (`tell_lock`, logging `lock asked for`); `lock_frames` sends the lock surfaces their frame callbacks, and while the lock holds no window gets one (`drm.rs`, `winit.rs`), so a game left running draws nothing for nobody; the state file says `locked`. Logs `screen locked` and `screen unlocked`.
- `edelshell.rs` (M5.3a): `edel-shell-v1`, our private protocol with shell-ui, generated by `wayland-scanner` from `protocols/edel-shell-v1.xml`, which shell-ui reads too. `Links` keeps each bound link with the policy it was told; `sync_shell`, run by `state_changed`, sends the shown workspace's policy when it changes; `toggle_policy` does what Super+Y does; `show_launcher` sends `launcher` to every link (M5.3b) and logs `launcher`; `show_tray` sends `tray` (the tray's key, Super+Shift+B, M5.9h) and logs `tray`; `show_styles` sends `styles` (the tiling styles' key, Super+Shift+Y, `Act::Styles`, M6.9) and logs `styles`; `tell_settings` (M5.9h) sends `settings` to every link, which `reload_settings` calls at its end, so shell-ui reads the files again, and logs nothing; `show_switcher` and `hide_switcher` send the switcher's titles and its end (M5.3c). `sync_shell` also sends `playing` (M7.7c, `playing_everywhere`) and `game` (M7.7b, `playing`: a game on some screen) when they change, and the request `on_battery` goes to `battery_changed` (`games.rs`). `tell_keyboard` (M5.9f) sends `keyboard` (the layouts' xkb names, comma separated, and the one in use) to each link whose last told value differs, at bind, after `apply_keyboard` and after Super+Space, and the request `next_keyboard_layout` does what Super+Space does. `media_key` sends the keyboard's volume, mute and brightness keys (`volume_up`, `volume_down`, `mute`, `brightness_up`, `brightness_down`, named by `shortcuts::media_key`) and logs `media key NAME` (M5.9c); `input.rs` keeps those keys from apps, as they mean one thing on every keyboard and are no shortcuts to rebind. The power key is `media_key` `power` (M7.8). `tell_lid` sends `lid` (1 closed, 0 opened) to every link (M7.8). `sync_shell` sends `touch_mode` (M9.6, `Edel::tablet_mode`) when it changes, which shell-ui tells apps as touch mode. The interface grows within version 1 until the first public release, as both ends ship together. `move_workspace(from, to)` (M5.2p) moves a workspace, as a drag of its switcher button asks; `toggle_overview` (M5.2j-b) shows or leaves the overview, as a click on the switcher's lit workspace asks. The `lock` event (M5.10a) asks shell-ui to lock the screen. `show_clipboard` (M7.13, Super+V, `Act::ClipboardHistory`) sends `clipboard` and logs `clipboard history`; the request `paste` is `clipboard.rs`'s `paste`. `screens_off` (M7.16b) calls `screens_off_asked`. The request `saver` (M8.31) goes to `saver_changed` (`tiers.rs`).
- `switcher.rs` (M5.3c): the window switcher, the compositor's own, so it works without shell-ui. `switcher_step` opens it on the window after the focused one, in the order of last use (the stacking order, top first, then the shown workspace's minimized windows), or steps it, back with Shift, sends at most ten titles around the chosen one and logs `switcher at T`; `switcher_done` switches to the chosen window (restoring it if minimized) when the keys' modifiers are let go, logging `switched to window T`, or not on Escape; `switcher_forget` drops a window that closed. `input.rs` forwards the modifiers' release to the app and finishes after it.
- `toplevels.rs` (M5.2d): wlr-foreign-toplevel-management, version 3, for the panel's window list and docks. `Toplevels`, in a `RefCell` on `Edel` as `state_changed` takes `&self`, keeps each bound manager and the handles it was given with what each was last told (`Seen`: title, a web app's without the browser's name as `decoration::title` gives it, app id, the client's `wl_output`s the window overlaps, or a minimized one's frame did, maximized, minimized and activated); `sync_toplevels`, run by `state_changed`, `focus` and `output_bound`, gives new windows a handle, sends what changed and `done`, and `closed` for windows gone. a new handle always gets its first `done`; a window minimized on the shown workspace whose screen went is on the screen it would come back on; `activate` brings a minimized window back, else shows a hidden window's workspace first, then focuses it; `close`, `set_maximized`, `unset_maximized`, `set_minimized` and `unset_minimized` act. Windows on hidden workspaces are on no screen.
- `workspaces.rs` (M5.2a, M5.2i, M5.2k): `switch_workspace` (Super+N) unmaps the shown windows into `desks` and maps the other workspace's back, stacked as they were, then lays them out, gives the keyboard to the top one (or nothing) and lets the pointer's focus follow (`repoint`); `move_to_workspace` (Super+Shift+N) sends the focused window, a maximized one at the size it goes back to, and returns whether it moved; `set_workspace_count` follows the count. M5.2i: `switch_neighbour` and `move_to_neighbour` (Super+Ctrl+Right and Left; Super+Ctrl+Shift+Right and Left, which follow the window), and `settle_dynamic`, which each window opening, closing or moving and each switch calls in dynamic mode: it applies `dynamic_plan` through `Desks::remove` and `add`, tells the clients and the state file, and logs `workspaces now N (dynamic)` when the count changes. A hidden window is deactivated; nothing happens during a drag. Logs `workspace N` and `window T to workspace N`. A switch is one spring per screen (M5.2f, M5.14): `begin_switch` starts it, `snapshot_leaving` keeps each shown window's last picture moving with it `desks::slide_by` off the screen, and the other workspace's windows are mapped at their places and carried in by it; on Lite it takes no time. Three fingers across a touchpad take the workspaces (`swipe_moved`, `swipe_ended`, `Swipe`, `Held`): the switch follows them one to one, goes on past half way or on a flick, else springs back to the workspace shown before; nothing else switches while they hold it. `minimize` (the bar's button, xdg-shell's request, the window list) hides a shown window as a closed one fades, the others filling its place in tiling, and logs `minimized window T`; `restore` shows its workspace, maps it back on top with the keyboard and logs `restored window T`. The state file says `minimized` for each window. With workspaces on each screen (M5.2k), `switch_workspace` switches the pointer's screen alone (`switch_screen`, logged `workspace N on SCREEN`), `switch_for` the screen of a window being brought forward, `note_screen` (on every pointer motion) makes the pointer's screen the one in use, `settle_screens` runs after a drop, a screen change and the key's change, and `set_per_screen` logs `workspaces per screen on|off`; the state file gives each output its `workspace`. `move_workspace` (M5.2p, `edel-shell-v1`'s `move_workspace` request from a drag in the switcher, or a frame dragged in the overview's strip) reorders, logs `workspace F moved to T` and writes the person's `workspaces.names` and `workspaces.wallpapers` in the new order (`keep_order`, or `messages::order_not_kept` and `order_no_home`), so a workspace takes its name and its wallpaper along.

- `input.rs`: three fingers swiped across move the workspaces one to one (`Edel::swipe_moved`, M5.14) and four fingers swiped up on a touchpad open the overview and down leave it (`Edel::swipe_ended`, M5.2j-b4); with `EDEL_LOG_KEYS=1` (CI's desktop test session alone, `Edel::log_keys`) each key's keysym, press or release, the modifiers held, the keys down and whether the overview is open are logged, so a key CI sends and the compositor never sees shows; keyboard, pointer, scrolling and pens (`zwp_tablet_v2`, M4.6b) for both backends (relative and absolute motion, click to focus and raise; `under` finds the frame part or surface at a point; a bar drags, double-clicks to maximize and its buttons act on release; edges resize; Super with the left or right button starts a move or resize; the keys `shortcuts.rs` binds act, but none of them but Super+L (`lock_screen`, so no app can keep the screen unlocked) while the focused surface holds an active shortcuts inhibitor (`everyday.rs`, M5.13b): then the terminals, the media keys and the keys of the lock screen still reach what they reach, and every other key goes to the app) and the terminal Ctrl+Alt+F1 to F12 asks for. Every input event notifies the idle notifier (`notify_activity`, M7.8). A lid switch (`SwitchToggle` with `Switch::Lid`) logs `lid closed` or `lid opened` and calls `tell_lid`; a tablet-mode switch (`Switch::TabletMode`, M9.6) logs `tablet mode on` or `tablet mode off`, keeps it in `Edel::tablet_mode` and calls `sync_shell`. Any input first turns the screens on again (`screens_on`, M7.16b).
- `touch.rs` (M9.5): touch screens. The first finger down is the pointer: it moves it where it touches and presses the left button through `Edel::button` (the one path a mouse click takes too) and tells the keyboard on the screen which window it tapped, a drag moves it with the button held, and lifting it releases the button, so a tap clicks wherever a click works and a drag on a title bar moves the window; other fingers wait while it is down. Apps get no wl_touch of their own yet.
- `screen_keyboard.rs` (M9.5): the keyboard on the screen, wvkbd (`wvkbd-mobintl`, the `screen-keyboard` feature). `Edel::follow_screen_keyboard` runs after every dispatch of the clients: when a field asks for text (smithay's active text-input-v3) in the client whose window a finger last tapped (`ScreenKeyboard::tapped`, from `touch.rs`), wvkbd is shown, started through `session::start_child` the first time with the tokens' colours and font (`args`) and sent SIGUSR2 after; once no field asks, or a real key is pressed, it gets SIGUSR1 and hides. A mouse or a key forgets the tap; taps on layer surfaces, the panels and the keyboard itself, do not count. Logs `screen keyboard shown` and `screen keyboard hidden`.
- `grabs.rs`: `WindowGrab`, one pointer grab for moving and resizing (`resized` keeps the opposite edges and a 96x64 minimum; a maximized window lets go after 8 px); it hands the final place to `Edel::placed` when the button is released.
- `statefile.rs`: the writer thread of `/run/edel/session/state.toml` (only outside a session it does nothing); the newest state wins. `replace` writes a file whole under `NAME.new` and renames it over the old one, never opening a file through a link; the health file `ready` is written the same way, since the greeter's compositor and the person's replace each other's files (M4.7b). Each `[[outputs]]` entry has `name`, `scale`, its place and logical size, and for Settings' Displays page (M5.7a) `mode` (the size in pixels in use), `modes` (every size the screen lists, `layout::mode_sizes`: each once, largest first), `preferred_mode` and `auto_scale` (the scale it gets when the settings file names none; the DRM backend adds each of the connector's modes to the output, `Gpu::light`).
- `program.rs` (M4.7b): `edel-compositor -- PROGRAM ARG...` starts the program once the first screen has shown a frame (in the development window, from the first turn of the event loop), with `WAYLAND_DISPLAY`, the X11 display as `DISPLAY` and the cursor's `XCURSOR_THEME` and `XCURSOR_SIZE`; a thread waits for it, and when it exits, or cannot start, the compositor stops. greetd's greeter is `edel-compositor -- edel-shell-ui --greeter --cmd /usr/libexec/edel-session` (M5.10b). `edel-compositor --game -- PROGRAM ARG...` is a game session (M8.26, `Program::game`): `greeter()` and `game_session()` tell the two apart; a game session starts shell-ui with `--game` (`shellui.rs`), which draws no panels or desktop widgets but keeps the lock screen and the idle ladder, writes the person's session log, starts the session's programs but not the startup apps or the welcome (`session.rs`), puts every window fullscreen as it maps (`state.rs`) and keeps game mode on (`Game::session`). `open` starts a program in the session the same way, without ending it, as Super+T does with foot (M5.13a); `run` starts a whole command, in the person's home folder, as the overview's search does (M5.2j-b3). `open_default` (M6.10) starts the app `[default_apps]` names (`Settings.default_apps`, the person's over the machine's), else the first app of the role's category (`edel::apps::default_app`), logging `opened default ROLE app ID`; with none it starts the fallback or logs `no ROLE app installed; set default_apps.ROLE` (`messages::no_default_app`). It reads the desktop files on each press.
- `dmabuf.rs` (M5.19, M7.7 `Offer` and `Feedbacks`, the scanout feedback `drm.rs` sends each screen's windows): `zwp_linux_dmabuf_v1`, version 4 with feedback, so apps hand over GPU buffers: `Dmabufs` (smithay's `DmabufState`, the global once offered, the backend's `Importer`); `offer_dmabuf` makes the global from the renderer's formats on the GPU's render node and logs `apps may hand over GPU buffers in N formats` (or that it offers none); `dmabuf_imported` checks a buffer by importing it once and logs the first window that draws on the GPU. The DRM backend offers it; winit does not.
- `games.rs` (M7.7, M7.7c, M7.7d): game mode on `Edel` (`game`, a `Game`: the rule and the battery as shell-ui reports it, `on_battery`, `battery_changed`, logged `game mode on|off (the charger)`); `follow_game_mode` follows the key from `reload_settings`; `toggle_game_mode` is Super+G (`Act::GameMode`); either change logs `game mode on|off (WHY)`, sends `game_mode` over `edel-shell-v1` (`tell_game_mode`), shown in shell-ui's pop-up, and draws again; the state file says `game_mode`. `fullscreen_top` is the fullscreen window on top of a screen (also `layers_over`'s test); `wants_vrr` is `playing_on`, the screen's fullscreen top window in game mode with nothing locking and no overview open (M7.7c). A game that plays (M7.7c): `playing` says so on any screen (the state file's `playing`, and the log `a game is playing; the desktop steps aside` or `the game ended; the desktop is back` from `state_changed`, once per change, `stepped_aside`), `playing_everywhere` on every screen (shell-ui's `playing` event, so a clock on another screen never stops), and `behind_game` marks a window that gets no frame callback while every screen it is on plays another window. tearing-control-v1 (`wp_tearing_control_manager_v1`, version 1): each surface's one control and hint, an `async` hint logged `a window asks to tear`; smithay 0.7 has no asynchronous page flips, so nothing tears: such a game draws unthrottled and each refresh shows its newest frame (Seam: tearing needs only `drm.rs` to pass the hint on once smithay flips asynchronously). Each game's smoothness (M7.7d): `follow_game_record`, run by `state_changed`, starts a `Recording` for the first screen's playing window and ends it when that changes; `Game::committed` (from `commit`) marks the game's new frame and `Game::frame_shown` (from `drm.rs`'s page flip, with its monotonic time) counts it in `edel::smoothness::Frames`; the end logs `game APP: N frames in S s, average F fps, slowest 1% X ms, slowest 0.1% Y ms` and replaces the app's record in the person's `games.toml` (`places::GAMES`) through a renamed `.new` file. The winit backend records nothing.
- `frame_rate.rs` (M8.27): the frame rate overlay. `toggle_frame_rate` is Super+Shift+G (`Act::FrameRate`, `toggle_frame_rate`), logged `the frame rate overlay on|off (Super+Shift+G)` until the next login, kept in `Game::overlay`; `frame_rate_elements`, which `render.rs` puts over a playing game's window under the cursor, paints the panel (`paint`: the figure and its unit, a graph of the last frame times against twice their average inside a track, and the line of the average and the slowest 1 percent, in the name pills' glass from the tokens) at most every 250 ms and only when its numbers change, from `Game::live` (`games.rs`' `Recording::recent`, fed with the record's frames), and logs `the frame rate overlay shows N fps, ...` at its first picture.
- `fullscreen.rs` (M5.20): xdg-shell's fullscreen: `fullscreen` (its own screen or the one it names), `unfullscreen` (back to its frame from before, maximized again if it was), `toggle_fullscreen` for Super+F; `FrameData::before_fullscreen` holds that frame, and a fullscreen window has no insets; `layers_over` gives only the overlays while a fullscreen window is its screen's top window, so the panel is neither drawn nor reached, and a window raised over it brings the panel back. The state file writes `fullscreen`, and wlr-foreign-toplevel reports and sets it.
- `keyboard.rs` (M5.21a): `apply_keyboard` hands `region.keyboard` (the settings' `keyboard`, parsed by `edel::keyboard`) to xkb with `set_xkb_config`, at start and when it changes, logging `keyboard layout NAME` or `keyboard layouts A, B`, or that a layout could not be loaded and the one in use stays; `next_keyboard_layout` (Super+Space) cycles and logs `keyboard layout now NAME`, xkb's name for it; both then call `tell_keyboard`, so shell-ui's indicator follows (M5.9f).
- `sharp.rs` (M8.28): sharp scaling for games. `Edel::scale_for`, which `render.rs`'s `elements` calls for each output, sets the renderer's upscale filter to nearest while the game playing on that output (`playing_on`) is shown at a whole number of times its drawn size (`upscaled`, from the surface's buffer scale and its viewport's source and destination), so each pixel the game drew becomes a square of the screen's, and back to linear otherwise; the choice is kept per output (`Sharp` in its user data) and logged `game on NAME scaled sharply, N times` and `pictures on NAME scaled smoothly again`. The window's size is not changed, so input needs nothing.
- `screencopy.rs` (M6.3): wlr-screencopy, version 3, shared memory only: `capture_output` and `capture_output_region` (the region in the output's logical coordinates, scaled to buffer pixels and clamped by `buffer_area`) tell the client its `Xrgb8888` buffer and wait for `copy` or `copy_with_damage`, which are checked (shared memory, the size sent, the stride), and the frame is filled at the next frame its output draws: `fill_copies`, which both backends call right after they draw an output, renders the same elements into an offscreen texture of the output's size, turned as the screen is (M9.8, a region turned into the glass's way up first), kept in `Edel::copy_textures` while that size stays, reads it back (`copy_framebuffer`, rows laid out as `TextureMapping::flipped` says) and `copy_area` writes the region's rows from the mapped readback straight into the client's pool honouring its stride, then `flags`, `damage` for `copy_with_damage` and `ready` with the monotonic clock; any error is `failed`, a frame whose client went away is dropped, and each client's first copy is logged `screen copied for pid N on OUTPUT WxH`. A cursor drawn on its plane (`Kind::Cursor`) is left out unless the client asked for `overlay_cursor`; one drawn in software is in every copy. Pure: `buffer_area` and `copy_area`, tested.
- `backend.rs` (the whole-system review of 2026-10-25): the one event loop. `run` makes the display and `Edel`, listens, opens each backend (an `Open`, `drm::open` or `winit::open`, which puts its own event sources in the loop) and, once per dispatch, asks every `Backend` to `scan` after a `[displays]` change (`Edel::screens_changed`) and to `render`; `--bench` stops it after 5 s and prints the telemetry. Seam (ADR-011): a virtual screen or a paired device's screen is one more `Backend` in that list.
- `drm.rs` (M4.2b, M4.6c): the backend for real screens. A libseat session opens the primary GPU (`primary_gpu`, else the first); GBM, EGL and `GlesRenderer`; `Gpu::scan` lights every connected screen the settings want (a `Screen`: its output, a CRTC of its own, a `DrmCompositor`) at `displays.NAME.resolution`, else its preferred mode, else the first it lists, darkens screens unplugged or turned off (taking their `wl_output` global away, so no app lists a screen nothing draws: grim failed on one, M6.3), and places them with `place_screens`; it runs at start, on udev's change events, when the seat comes back and when `Edel::screens_changed` is set by a change to `[displays]`; libinput on the session. Windows open on the first screen (`output_area`) until M5.2g; workspaces span every screen. Each screen draws when it is dirty (`Edel::dirty` marks them all) and its last frame's vblank has come, and what it drew fills the screen copies asked of it (`fill_copies`, M6.3); on the vblank, presentation feedback goes to clients, each screen's first frame logs `edel-compositor: output NAME WxH ready`, and the first screen to draw writes `/run/edel/session/ready`, the desktop's health (M4.8). With no screen connected at start the compositor stays up, writes `ready` as `no screen connected yet; waiting for one` and lights the screen udev later reports, so a desktop booted without its screen is never restarted by the boot guard. A timer armed by the first frame after a quiet spell logs the telemetry once the screen has been still for 2 s, so nothing wakes while idle. Pausing the session (another VT) pauses DRM and libinput; activating resets the compositor's state and redraws. It logs the screen driver at start; on a firmware framebuffer or a plain virtual card (`draws_whole`: simpledrm, efidrm, vesadrm, bochs, cirrus), which copy only the areas a frame names onto one picture of their own, it draws every frame whole (M3.6); game mode (M7.7): each screen says when lit whether it `has variable refresh` (only `Supported`, which needs no mode change, is used), `follow_vrr` turns it on before a frame when `Edel::wants_vrr` says so and off after, logging `variable refresh on|off on NAME` (a refusal is `messages::vrr_refused` and the screen is not asked again), and presentation feedback then says `Variable`; each screen has `dmabuf::Feedbacks` (`Offer::feedbacks`: the renderer's formats, and a scanout tranche of the primary plane's formats on the display device), sent to its windows after each frame through `select_dmabuf_feedback`, so a fullscreen window that takes a shown format goes straight to the screen, and each change of the fullscreen window on top is logged `scanout=direct for window T on NAME` or `scanout=unavailable for window T on NAME (WHY)`; the session waits up to 10 s for seatd (`open_seat`) and up to 10 s for a card's file (`wait_for_gpu`): the first card whose file exists, udev's primary first, then udev's others, then `/dev/dri`'s own files (`pick_gpu`), as the firmware's framebuffer can hand the screen to the real driver as `card1` while udev still names `card0`. Each draw's frame callbacks skip a window behind a game (`behind_game`) and give the layers only what `layers_over` names on a screen a game plays on (M7.7c). Screens turned off for nobody's use (M7.16b, `Screen::off`, `Edel::screens_off`): a screen not drawing waits for its queued frame, is cleared once (`clear`, DPMS off, planes off) and logs `screens off, N cleared`; while it is off no frame is drawn and no frame callback goes to its windows, and a locked lock is confirmed on it (`lock_drawn`); at the next input it draws a whole frame and lights again.
- `xwayland.rs` (M4.7): X11 apps. `listen` takes the first free X11 display (its lock file `/tmp/.XN-lock`, as X servers write it, taken over when its process is gone; its socket in the abstract namespace and in `/tmp/.X11-unix`) and logs `X11 apps on :N`, if `xwayland-satellite` is on the `PATH` (feature `xwayland`). When the first X11 app connects, `xwayland-satellite :N -listenfd A -listenfd B` starts with copies of the sockets (`rustix::io::dup`, so they survive exec), runs XWayland on them, and turns X11 windows into ordinary Wayland windows, so they get our bars, policies and keyboard focus with no X11 code here. While it runs the sockets' sources rest; a thread waits for it, and when it stops, apps still waiting are turned away and the sockets wait again, so the next X11 app starts it anew and a crash never loops. The lock and socket go when the compositor exits. No smithay XWayland feature: smithay 0.7 opens XWayland's sockets itself, so it could not start on demand.
- `winit.rs`: the development backend, built only with the `winit` feature (M6.17), out of the product, one `Backend` in `backend.rs`'s loop. A frame is drawn only when `dirty` is set (a commit, a new or closed window, input that focuses, a resize), so an idle desktop draws nothing; what it draws fills the screen copies asked of it (`fill_copies`, M6.3); `--bench` stops after 5 s and prints the telemetry. Its frame callbacks follow `drm.rs`'s behind a game (M7.7c).
